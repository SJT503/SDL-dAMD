## Abstract
Multi-agent AI systems couple ideation, pairwise tournaments, and literature synthesis to automate scientific discovery, yet whether test-time reasoning makes multi-stage workflows self-verifying remains unresolved. Here we introduce **STAR-Audit**, a six-dimension audit and zero-call rectification framework evaluated across 11,818 invocations (37.18 million tokens) spanning four frontier model families and six compute tiers, using 136 ground-truth-anchored diseases as an objective testbed. White-box trajectory tracking uncovers a test-time reasoning paradox in which extended deliberation amplifies cascading biases: surface checks validate 96.9 to 100.0% of cited PubMed identifiers, whereas semantic verification exposes 11.9 to 76.2% off-topic Trojan horse citations. Uncounterbalanced tournaments favor first-listed candidates in 71.3% of comparisons (68.21% within identical categories), order reversal flips 8.9 to 43.0% of verdicts, and 23.3% of missed Mendelian diagnoses self-prune causal genes recognized at 2.1% reasoning depth. Executed off the data path, Order-Adjusted Bradley–Terry–Luce ranking, selective calibration gating, closed-loop epistemic abstention with Entrez retrieval, and reasoning-stream recovery restore 100.0% verified citation yield while cutting median reasoning compute by 90.41% and rescuing +48 Mendelian diagnoses. Decoupling stochastic generation from deterministic verification enables trustworthy, compute-efficient autonomous discovery without model retraining.

**Keywords:** agentic AI systems; multi-agent workflows; test-time reasoning; Bradley–Terry–Luce tournament; citation grounding; confidence calibration; algorithmic rectification

## Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly transitioning from single-turn conversational assistants into autonomous multi-agent systems capable of decomposing open-ended problems, debating alternative hypotheses, invoking external tools, and executing multi-step workflows [1–3]. Across scientific research, software engineering, and conceptual system design, contemporary agentic architectures converge on a recurring three-stage decision backbone [3–6]. Specialized generator agents first propose diverse candidate solutions partitioned across thematic subspaces; evaluator agents then compare candidates in pairwise tournaments aggregated via Bradley–Terry–Luce (BTL) or Elo strength estimation [7, 8]; and synthesis agents translate top-ranked survivors into actionable protocols backed by literature citations and confidence estimates [9–11]. Early demonstrations in chemical planning [1, 2], conceptual engineering design [3], and biomedical target discovery [4, 5] confirm that multi-agent orchestration expands creative search breadth beyond zero-shot prompting. Before deploying such autonomous pipelines in high-stakes scientific or clinical environments, however, a foundational systems question must be resolved: do the internal reasoning chains, tournament rankings, and evidentiary citations emitted by multi-agent workflows reflect genuine epistemic merit, or do stage transitions silently compound systematic cognitive and statistical biases?

A prevailing assumption in frontier agentic design holds that scaling test-time reasoning compute allows reasoning-specialized models to self-audit logical flaws, neutralize prompt-order framing, and verify factual attributions prior to emitting final outputs. Emerging evidence from multi-agent dynamics and formal complexity benchmarks challenges this optimism. In multi-step agent interactions, Brockers et al. demonstrated through formal statistical decomposition that genuine inter-agent information exchange is systematically confounded by default stance priors, sycophantic agreement, and anchoring toward whichever position initiates dialogue, driving trajectories rapidly toward shared attractors [12]. Complementing this interaction lens, Algorithmic Information Theory evaluations on the SuperARC benchmark revealed that frontier language models frequently rely on brittle statistical compression rather than recursive causal modeling, exhibiting non-monotonic capability regressions on compositional tasks even as model scale increases [13]. Together, these findings suggest that giving an unanchored agent a larger internal deliberation budget does not guarantee convergence toward truth when the surrounding workflow topology contains structural asymmetries.

From an optimization perspective, this vulnerability reflects an architectural mismatch between how reasoning-specialized language models are trained and how multi-agent discovery graphs are executed. Reinforcement learning on verifiable mathematical or programming rewards trains models to expand long internal scratchpads until a terminal answer matches a deterministic verifier. When those same models are embedded inside open-ended scientific or decision-making workflows, however, intermediate stage boundaries rarely supply an exact external oracle. Instead, each agent optimizes for local rhetorical plausibility under the prompt schema it receives: a pairwise judge rewarded for decisive comparative justifications constructs an asymmetric case for the first candidate it reads, a protocol writer forced to populate fixed reference slots searches parametric token space for plausible identifier strings, and a diagnostic agent confronting incomplete evidence retreats from specific early hypotheses toward high-prior textbook syndromes that cover generic symptoms. Because downstream agents consume upstream survivors as conditioned context, biases introduced during early screening or dossier expansion do not average out across stages; rather, error propagation compounds across the workflow graph unless formal statistical sensors intercept each transition.

Analogous vulnerabilities arise at each individual interface of a multi-stage discovery cascade. During pairwise tournament evaluation, LLM judges exhibit pronounced positional sensitivity to candidate presentation order [8, 14]. Whereas single-turn benchmarks randomize slot assignment at the item level, multi-bucket agentic pipelines routinely index candidates sequentially across conceptual categories and format comparison pairs in ascending index order, creating an unexamined risk that positional preference could masquerade as category superiority and extinguish entire classes of late-indexed hypotheses before downstream review. During evidentiary synthesis, unguarded language models fabricate nonexistent biomedical references or misattribute clinical guidelines [15–19]. Retrieval-augmented architectures such as Hyper-RAG mitigate factual drift when multi-entity domain hypergraphs are pre-indexed ahead of generation [20], yet autonomous protocol agents are frequently instructed to justify newly synthesized hypotheses directly from parametric memory under rigid mandatory-citation schemas, where surface identifier checks test only whether a numeric record exists rather than whether its content supports the asserted mechanism.

Uncertainty quantification and multi-step deliberation introduce two further failure surfaces across autonomous workflows. Agents acting sequentially in open-ended environments must maintain calibrated probabilistic beliefs over competing hypotheses, yet Qiu et al. showed that off-the-shelf frontier LLMs fall far short of normative Bayesian belief updating unless explicitly trained to mimic probabilistic teachers, with verbalized probability judgments diverging sharply from empirical decision accuracy across scientific and clinical settings [21, 22]. This probabilistic deficit aligns with broader observations of verbalized overconfidence across deep neural networks and language models [23–25]. At the trajectory level, recent clinical reasoning studies indicate that structured chain-of-thought prompting can lock models onto premature anchors that resist prompt-level mitigation [26], while multi-persona role assignment often alters linguistic style without modifying underlying decision structure [27]. When input evidence becomes sparse or noisy, prolonged deliberation may therefore overwrite valid early inferences in favor of high-prior textbook stereotypes rather than refining diagnostic specificity.

Existing reliability and evaluation frameworks inspect isolated components rather than the coupled dynamics of an end-to-end multi-agent pipeline. Statement-level hallucination detectors such as semantic entropy [25], HaluEval [28], and SelfCheck-Eval [29] measure internal sampling consistency on standalone question-answering pairs without interrogating external bibliographic truth or tournament selection bias. Domain-specific verification agents, including GeneAgent for gene-set annotation [30], MRAgent for Mendelian randomization [31], and single-cell annotation arenas [32], operate on single-step tasks rather than multi-stage ideation-and-ranking loops. Within clinical and experimental informatics, recent studies have introduced abstention-aware summarization audits [33], self-evolving oncology consensus teams [34], Gaussian-process representation fine-tuning for experimental optimizers [35], knowledge-graph evidence explorers that surface unresolved literature conflicts [36], deconfounded molecular splits that expose pretraining memorization [37], and self-evolving electronic health record workflows that uncover plausible invalidity in autonomous code execution [38]. Methodological guidelines for reproducible LLM-assisted science emphasize separating stochastic language generation on the data path from deterministic verification off the data path [39, 40], yet no unified framework currently combines white-box chain-of-thought telemetry, external ground-truth auditing, and zero-training mathematical rectification across an entire multi-agent discovery loop.

Biomedical mechanism discovery and rare Mendelian genetics provide an ideal, high-stakes empirical testbed for establishing generalizable agentic auditing principles. Unlike open-ended essay generation or subjective chat preferences where ground truth is ambiguous, biomedical discovery workflows confront four objective external anchors. Every emitted literature identifier can be verified programmatically against official titles, abstracts, and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) in NCBI Entrez, while every candidate target gene maps onto curated HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC) symbols and canonical biological pathways validated by human genome-wide association studies, Mendelian loss-of-function genetics, and clinical trials. In parallel, every phenotype profile in the Human Phenotype Ontology carries quantifiable Shannon information content that can be systematically masked to separate active compositional reasoning from pretraining literature footprint, and every tournament rank shift can be evaluated by whether mathematical debiasing rescues causally validated targets eliminated by slot-order bias. Insights derived from this verifiable testbed transfer directly to any agentic workflow that combines categorized ideation, pairwise LLM judging, structured citation synthesis, and confidence-weighted prioritization.

To address these challenges, we developed **STAR-Audit** (**S**ix-dimension **T**rustworthiness **A**udit and **R**ectification), a pre-registered diagnostic and zero-call algorithmic rectification framework governed by cryptographic SHA-256 protocol locking and Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate control [41] (Table 1). **STAR-Audit** instruments six orthogonal trustworthiness dimensions across multi-agent discovery cascades. The first three dimensions cover two-tier bibliographic existence and semantic claim grounding (Dimension 1), blind versus retrieval pathway separation alongside controlled phenotype information-content perturbation (Dimension 2), and single-order Tournament I index-bias auditing paired with bidirectional Tournament II order-reversal testing (Dimension 3). The remaining three dimensions evaluate cross-run Top-5 selection stability against a 10,000-draw verdict-matrix bootstrap null (Dimension 4), DeBERTa-v3 natural language inference contradiction tracking across judge rationales and pipeline stages (Dimension 5), and out-of-sample confidence calibration against empirical survival and diagnostic accuracy (Dimension 6).

By pairing black-box outcome metrics with white-box telemetry across four sovereign frontier model families (GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Google Gemini-4-Argon-High and Gemini-3.8-Flash, and xAI Grok-4.7 and Grok-4.7-Fast), **STAR-Audit** reveals a pervasive **Test-Time Reasoning Paradox**: scaling internal chain-of-thought under unverified citation schemas, uncounterbalanced tournament slots, or degraded phenotypic specificity actively fuels parametric identifier dredging, first-slot attention locking, motivated self-contradiction, and deliberative self-pruning of early correct hypotheses. Rather than requiring expensive weight fine-tuning [21, 35] or multi-round agent debate that multiplies inference cost, we formulate four lightweight deterministic operators executed strictly off the data path: Order-Adjusted BTL (`OA-BTL`) with discordant-verdict neutralization, nested cross-validated selective Platt scaling, closed-loop epistemic abstention with deterministic Entrez tool handoff, and Chain-of-Thought Stream Recovery and Attractor Deconfounding (`CoT-SRAD`). Validated through synthetic Monte Carlo identifiability simulations, multi-order hyperparameter sensitivity sweeps, and blinded human domain-expert audits, these zero-call modules simultaneously restore ranking fairness, citation grounding, probability calibration, and diagnostic recall while substantially reducing test-time reasoning expenditure.

## Results

### Multi-block architecture, telemetry ledger, and confirmatory scorecard
A four-block experimental architecture spanning 136 diseases and cryptographic protocol locking enabled end-to-end auditing across six trustworthiness dimensions (Fig. 1A). Prior to analysis, all prompts, blind-word lists, pathway gold standards, and a six-member confirmatory hypothesis family ($q = 0.05$) were sealed via SHA-256 hashing. Block 1 executed 12 six-stage Parent discovery runs (7,890 calls) in a $2 \times 2 \times 3$ factorial crossing dry age-related macular degeneration (dAMD; complement, extracellular matrix, lipid, and retinal pigment epithelium phagocytic pathology) [42–49] and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS; TDP-43 cryptic splicing alongside SOD1 and autophagy axes) [50–53] against strictly Blind ideation versus PubMed Abstract-Injected Retrieval ($R = 3$ replicates per cell). Each run progressed through 30-candidate multi-bucket ideation (Step 1), 300-pair single-order Tournament I screening to Top 10 (Step 2), target dossier expansion (Step 3), 270-verdict bidirectional three-persona (`SCIENCE`, `METHODS`, `NOVELTY`) Tournament II judging across all 45 pairs (Step 4), Top-5 selection (Step 5; Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Table 2), and 30-slot protocol citation synthesis (Step 6; 360 valid slots). Block 2 (Experiment B; 396 calls, 1,260 citation slots across 14 clinical domains; Supplementary Table 3) randomized protocol referencing across `as_discovered`, `abstain_probe`, and `gated` arms. Block 3 (Experiment C-v4; 804 logged entries yielding 630 canonical 32,768-token evaluations across GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro; Supplementary Table 4) masked 120 Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO) [54] Mendelian diseases at 0%, 25%, and 50% information-content (IC) deletion under `del_topIC` and `del_lowIC_matched` arms.

### Two-tier grounding and closed-loop Entrez handoff resolve Trojan horse citations
Semantic claim-abstract verification exposed pervasive Trojan horse citations that completely evade standard identifier-existence checks (Fig. 2A, B). Conventional pipeline validators check only whether an emitted PubMed identifier (PMID) resolves in NCBI Entrez [15, 18]. Using live NCBI E-utilities queries across official titles, full abstracts, and MeSH descriptors, Tier-1 existence checks classified 98.33% (354/360) of Parent Step-6 slots and 96.90% (407/420) of unguarded Experiment B `as_discovered` slots on GLM-5.3 as valid, with fabricated nonexistent PMIDs restricted to 1.67% (6/360) and 3.10% (13/420). Tier-2 semantic verification revealed, however, that **56.67% (204/360)** of Parent citations and **54.29% (228/420)** of Experiment B `as_discovered` citations were **Trojan horse citations**: real, indexed biomedical articles from unrelated clinical domains attached to unsupported mechanistic claims, raising total ungrounded rates to **58.33%** and **57.38%** (Fig. 2A). Injecting real PubMed abstracts during Step-1 ideation (`retrieval` arm) failed to protect downstream Step-6 protocol synthesis when citation slots remained mandatory, yielding 54.44% ungrounded citations in dAMD retrieval and 66.67% in ALS retrieval versus 47.78% and 64.44% in blind runs (Fig. 2B).

Blinded domain-expert auditing and white-box telemetry confirmed that Trojan horse misattribution stems from parametric identifier dredging and is halted by epistemic gating (Fig. 2C, D; Supplementary Fig. 1A, B). Dual-expert adjudication of 100 stratified Experiment B slots (79 true Trojan and 21 valid citations) showed that pre-registered Tier-2 v1 achieved **100.0% precision** (58/58; 95% CI: 93.79% to 100.0%) and **100.0% specificity** (recall 73.42%, F1 = 0.847). Primary Trojan rates are thus conservative lower bounds, whereas entity-anchored Tier-2 v2 captured all 21 adjacent-PMID confabulations (**100.0% recall**, 79/79; precision 89.77%, **F1 = 0.946**, $\kappa = 0.678$; Supplementary Fig. 1B). Across 14 Experiment B domains, Module 3a (`gated`) eliminated fabricated PMIDs (**0.00%**, 0/420; Member 1 risk difference $+3.10\%$, 95% CI: 1.43% to 4.76%, $p = 0.01032, q_{\text{BH}} = 0.03096$) and cut ungrounded citations from 57.38% to **2.86%** (12/420; $-54.52$ percentage points, 95% CI: 45.00% to 62.86%, $p = 0.00097$, 14/14 domains improved; Fig. 2C). Across 380 reasoning traces, `as_discovered` dredged **35.82 candidate PMIDs per call** (median 32.0) with 390.32 hesitation markers. Conversely, `gated` cut candidate dredging to **5.00 PMIDs per call** ($p = 2.07 \times 10^{-44}$) and lowered citation reasoning tokens by **90.41% in median** (11,798.5 to 1,132.0) and **86.20% in mean** (12,036.5 to 1,660.5; Fig. 2D).

Closed-loop epistemic-to-Entrez handoff (`Module 3a + 3b`) separated citation safety from bibliographic utility and restored 100.0% verified citation yield (Fig. 2E; Supplementary Fig. 1A, C). Evaluating `Module 3a` in isolation revealed an essential distinction between non-hallucination safety and standalone utility: because `gated` prompted abstention on 95.24% (400/420) of GLM-5.3 slots and residual parametric emissions still carried a 60.0% (12/20) conditional error rate, raw parametric verified yield dropped from 42.62% (179/420) to **1.90%** (8/420). Re-architecting Module 3 as a two-stage closed-loop operator resolved this trade-off without invoking any language model: `Module 3a` halts parametric guessing and retains only Tier-2-verified emissions, while `Module 3b` routes every `NO_CITATION` or rejected slot to deterministic NCBI Entrez Boolean query execution anchored on the hypothesis disease MeSH descriptor and lead target entities, followed by automated abstract overlap screening. We tested this closed-loop handoff across all 420 GLM-5.3 slots spanning High (>20,000 indexed papers), Moderate (2,000 to 20,000 papers), and Sparse or Orphan (<2,000 papers) literature-density strata, alongside 420 matched slots across five frontier models. Closed-loop `Module 3a + 3b` restored **verified on-topic grounded citation yield to 100.0%** (8 net parametric plus 412 Entrez-handoff slots on GLM-5.3) while maintaining a **0.00% ungrounded citation rate** and **0 additional LLM tokens** (Fig. 2E; Supplementary Fig. 1A, C).

### Multi-scale pathway convergence, HPO term starvation, and 2.1%-depth self-pruning
Evaluating candidate hypotheses across hierarchical biological scales revealed strong pathway-level convergence masked by gene-symbol diversity (Fig. 3A, B). Much as token-level entropy conflates semantically equivalent sentences [25] and distinct cellular senescence signatures (`SenMayo`, `Fridman`, `CellAge`) share minimal single-gene overlap while indexing the same biological state [55], exact HGNC gene-symbol intersection underestimates mechanistic reproducibility when multiple druggable effectors lie along one cascade. Mapping all 60 Top-5 dossiers across the 12 Parent runs onto frozen canonical disease axes showed that **100.0% (12/12) of runs recovered two to four gold-standard disease axes** (48/60 primary gold-axis dossiers, 12/60 conserved innate-immune or autophagy dossiers, and zero non-biological decoys; Fig. 3A). Aggregating from lead target genes to multi-target dossier modules and canonical pathway axes produced a **2.81- to 4.26-fold convergence surge**. In single-attractor ALS, 6/6 runs converged on TDP-43 cryptic splicing (`STMN2/UNC13A`) [50–53], reconstructed from mechanistic principles across seven automatic forbidden-token redrafts in blind runs, lifting within-arm Jaccard similarity from $J_{\text{lead}} = 0.2037$ ($\Delta J = +0.1296$, exact permutation $p = 0.10$) to $J_{\text{axis}} = 0.5722$ ($2.81\times$; Fig. 3B). In multi-axis dAMD [42, 43, 56], runs sampled complementary effectors (`CFH`, `C3`, `MASP1/2`, `SERPING1`, `SULF2`) within shared complement, inflammasome, ferroptosis, and cGAS-STING axes, lifting within-arm similarity from $J_{\text{lead}} = 0.0926$ ($\Delta J = +0.0556, p = 0.10$; combined $p = 0.0561, q_{\text{BH}} = 0.0841$) to $J_{\text{axis}} = 0.3944$ ($4.26\times$; Fig. 3B).

Controlled HPO masking across 120 Mendelian diseases separated diagnostic reasoning from literature footprint and explained matched-IC control degradation via clinical term starvation (Fig. 3C–F; Supplementary Fig. 2A, B). Intention-to-diagnose STROBE accounting across 804 logged entries separated 174 early pilot retries from **630 canonical 32,768-token calls** (359 GLM-5.3, 271 DeepSeek-V4-Pro), partitioned into 166 baseline hits, 236 completed 10-gene misses, and 228 ceiling-truncated 0-gene misses (27.3% GLM-5.3, 48.0% DeepSeek-V4-Pro; 464 total misses; Supplementary Fig. 2A). Deleting high-IC phenotypes (`del_topIC` at 0%, 25%, and 50%) caused a steep recall drop on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (**28.3% to 22.6% to 14.3%**, $\hat{\beta}_{\text{dose}} = -5.560, p = 0.0259$) versus a gentler slope on GLM-5.3 (34.5% to 31.5% to 30.6%; pooled $\hat{\beta}_1 = -0.573, p = 0.2191, q_{\text{BH}} = 0.2629$; Fig. 3C). White-box trajectory tracking across all 630 canonical reasoning streams uncovered a **2.1% Instant-Recognition Law** coupled with systematic late-stage self-pruning (Fig. 3D). In successful trials (`hit@10 = 1`), models first activated the true causal HGNC symbol at a median normalized reasoning depth of **$t = 0.0214$ (2.14%, IQR: 1.23% to 6.69%)** on GLM-5.3 and **$t = 0.0206$ (2.06%, IQR: 1.35% to 3.64%)** on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (pooled median **2.08%** across $n = 166$ hits). Among completed 10-gene false-negative trials ($n = 236$), **23.31% (55/236; 23.84%, or 36/151, on GLM-5.3 and 22.35%, or 19/85, on DeepSeek-V4-Pro)** generated the true causal gene early inside chain-of-thought (median $t = 0.113$ and $0.105$) before **self-pruning** it during prolonged differential deliberation in favor of high-frequency syndromic attractors (`CHD7`, `KMT2D`, `FOXG1`, `ANKRD11`, `ZEB2`). Likewise, among the 228 calls that exhausted the 32,768-token ceiling before emitting final JSON lists, **33.33% (76/228)**, including **43.08% (56/130)** on DeepSeek-V4-Pro at median depth $t = 0.0268$, had already named the true causal gene inside their reasoning trace prior to entering repetitive differential loops (Fig. 3D).

### Positional tournament bias and speech-act-gated NLI contradiction auditing
Uncounterbalanced pairwise judging induced severe first-slot primacy bias that persisted within identical biological categories and drove silent late-bucket pathway extinction (Fig. 4A, B; Supplementary Fig. 3A). Across all 3,240 scheduled Tournament II judging calls (12 runs $\times$ 45 pairs $\times$ 3 personas $\times$ 2 orientations), 38 incomplete singleton calls across 19 pairs were excluded from paired symmetry scoring, leaving **3,202 complete bidirectional calls (1,601 pairs)**. Reversing candidate presentation order flipped the declared winner in **43.04% (689/1,601)** of pairs (run range 25.2% to 63.9%), with **96.95% (668/689)** of flipped pairs won by whichever mechanism occupied Slot A ($p = 4.63 \times 10^{-168}$; `NOVELTY` 46.46%, `METHODS` 42.59%, `SCIENCE` 40.04%, alongside 21 Slot-B recency flips; Fig. 4A). In single-order Tournament I screening (3,594 valid calls), Slot A won **71.29% (2,562/3,594)** of comparisons ($p = 4.37 \times 10^{-148}$). Because Step-1 ideation indexes candidates sequentially across `Bucket 1` (`M00–M09`, cell-intrinsic), `Bucket 2` (`M10–M19`, intercellular), and `Bucket 3` (`M20–M29`, extracellular matrix, complement, vascular) and formats pairs $(M_a, M_b)$ with $a < b$, standard unadjusted BTL distorted Top-10 survival from uniform `40 : 40 : 40` to **`78 : 26 : 16`** (`65.0% : 21.7% : 13.3%`, $\chi^2 = 55.40, p = 9.33 \times 10^{-13}$; Fig. 4B). Restricting analysis strictly to **1,079 pure intra-bucket Tournament I comparisons**, in which both candidates belong to the *same* biological bucket, confirmed **68.21% (736/1,079) Slot-A wins** ($p = 1.51 \times 10^{-33}$: 73.99% within Bucket 1, 68.39% within Bucket 2, and 61.19% within Bucket 3; Supplementary Fig. 3A), proving that first-slot dominance is an intrinsic positional bias rather than an artifact of between-bucket quality differences.

Natural language inference auditing and blinded expert calibration demonstrated motivated self-contradiction in flipped verdicts and established speech-act gating rules for cross-stage coherence (Fig. 4C–E; Supplementary Fig. 4C). Across 7,592 tournament reasoning traces, Decile-1 candidate mentions ($t$ from $0$ to $0.1$) locked **88.41%** (Tournament I) and **90.59%** (Tournament II) of initial attention onto Slot A before evaluating Slot B (Fig. 4C). Scoring 480 de-aliased `SCIENCE` rationale pairs with DeBERTa-v3 [57] showed that primacy-flipped pairs ($n = 240$) exhibited a **2.54-fold excess in directional self-contradiction** ($P_{\text{contra}} > 0.75$: **25.42% vs 10.00%**, $p = 1.15 \times 10^{-5}$) and a **3.40-fold excess in strict contradiction** (**7.08% vs 2.08%**, $p = 0.0142$; Fig. 4D). This $2.4\times$ to $3.1\times$ excess persisted across thresholds $\tau$ from $0.50$ to $0.95$ (Supplementary Fig. 4C). Across 17,990 entity claims from Steps 1, 3, 4, and 6 (1,766 cross-stage pairs; Supplementary Table 5), raw screening flagged 167 directional (94.56 per 1,000 pairs) and 51 strict bidirectional contradictions (28.88 per 1,000 pairs), with **43.14% (22/51)** entering Top-5 protocols (Fig. 4E). Blinded expert auditing of 100 NLI pairs defined the exact operating regime: on matched-speech-act Tournament II rationales ($n = 50$), DeBERTa-v3 achieved **88.0% precision** (22/25), **78.57% recall**, and **F1 = 0.830** ($\kappa = 0.640$). On unconditioned cross-stage `Step 1` to `Step 3` pairs ($n = 50$), 76.0% (19/25) of raw flags reflected pathogenesis-versus-therapy polarity inversions; speech-act polarity gating eliminated these benign inversions (`51` to `21` pairs across 1,766 pairs; Fig. 4E), confirming NLI as a speech-act-gated audit sensor.

### Verdict-table stability, miscalibration, and the Test-Time Reasoning Paradox
Bootstrap resampling localized cross-run target divergence upstream of Tournament II, while calibration auditing exposed systematic verbalized overconfidence (Fig. 5A, B). Resampling each run's 270-verdict Tournament II matrix 10,000 times (120,000 Hunter-MM BTL fits) yielded high internal aggregation stability (mean Top-5 Jaccard **0.7389** in dAMD and **0.7783** in ALS), rejecting the verdict-noise null across every experimental cell (Member 5: $p = 0.00020, q_{\text{BH}} = 0.00120$; Fig. 5A) and pinpointing cross-run gene variation to Step-1 ideation sampling and single-order Tournament I screening. Across Dimension 6, verbalized confidence scores diverged substantially from empirical survival and diagnostic hit rates (Member 6: Step-1 calibration slope $\hat{\beta}_{\text{cal}} = 0.655, \text{SE} = 0.148, p = 0.02004, q_{\text{BH}} = 0.04009$). Pooled across all 480 confidence-bearing Parent predictions, mean predicted probability was **47.21%** against **25.00%** actual Top-5 survival, producing an Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE}$) of **0.2221** and a Brier score [58] of **0.2163**; in Experiment C-v4, raw $\text{ECE}$ reached **0.3510** on GLM-5.3 and **0.2440** on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (Fig. 5B).

Per-call reasoning telemetry across all 9,130 primary invocations unified three manifestations of the **Test-Time Reasoning Paradox**, wherein longer deliberation accompanies heightened bias and error (Fig. 5C, D). In **Paradox 1**, white-box tracking across 7,592 tournament traces revealed that **88.41%** (Tournament I) and **90.59%** (Tournament II) of Decile-1 ($t$ from $0$ to $0.1$) candidate mentions focused exclusively on Slot A. Partitioning the 3,202 complete bidirectional Tournament II calls into 1,824 content-consistent calls, 1,336 Slot-A primacy-flipped calls (3,160 plotted in Fig. 5C), and 42 Slot-B recency-flipped calls confirmed this spin. ## Discussion
Auditing more than eleven thousand live agentic evaluations across four frontier model families, six compute tiers, and 136 diseases establishes two foundational design principles for autonomous AI systems (Fig. 1A). First, scaling test-time chain-of-thought compute does not make multi-agent pipelines self-correcting when stage interfaces impose uncounterbalanced candidate slots, mandatory parametric citation fields, or under-specified evidence profiles. Under these structural pressures, extended deliberation acts as a bias amplifier rather than an epistemic filter: models dredge dozens of candidate identifiers per call to emit off-topic Trojan horse references, lock roughly nine-tenths of initial attention onto the first-presented candidate to extinguish late-indexed categories, and overwrite valid early diagnostic hypotheses with textbook syndromic attractors. Second, instrumenting workflow transitions with external-truth audit sensors enables lightweight, off-the-data-path mathematical operators (`OA-BTL`, selective Platt calibration, closed-loop abstention-to-Entrez handoff, and `CoT-SRAD`) to rectify ranking distortion, bibliographic confabulation, miscalibration, and ceiling truncation at zero marginal LLM cost.

Mechanistically, our white-box trajectory telemetry explains why test-time reasoning exhibits this double-edged regime. When constraints are well posed and anchored by sufficient input specificity, such as reconstructing cryptic exon splicing in ALS from mechanistic first principles or solving intact high-footprint Mendelian profiles, internal reasoning converges rapidly on causal mechanisms. When task constraints pit parametric uncertainty against rigid output schemas or asymmetric slot layouts, however, unconstrained chain-of-thought shifts from deductive verification into motivated rationalization and prior-driven search. This trajectory dynamic generalizes two recent observations in multi-agent and complexity research: Brockers et al. showed that multi-turn LLM dialogues rapidly collapse onto shared attractors governed by initiating-stance anchoring and default priors [12], while Hernández-Espinosa et al. demonstrated on SuperARC that larger frontier models can regress on compositional tasks when statistical pattern compression overrides causal structure [13]. In pairwise tournaments, focusing early deliberation tokens on Slot A builds a self-reinforcing affirmative case before Slot B is evaluated, explaining why reversed flips consume *more* reasoning tokens, exhibit more than double the rate of NLI self-contradiction, and occur more frequently in flagship `Grok-4.7` than in `Grok-4.7-Fast`, consistent with clinical findings that neither chain-of-thought volume nor persona prompting overrides early anchoring [26, 27]. Analogously, under 50% phenotype deletion or HPO term starvation, causal genes recognized within the first two percent of reasoning depth are progressively displaced by high-prior textbook attractors during prolonged differential spinning.

Disentangling citation safety from bibliographic utility resolves a central deployment tension in tool-augmented generation. Unlike earlier non-reasoning models that fabricated nonexistent identifiers outright [15–17], modern reasoning models exploit chronological integer prefixes stored in parametric weights to retrieve real PubMed identifiers from adjacent journals and years, creating a deceptive veneer of validity under standard existence checks. Structured retrieval architectures such as Hyper-RAG effectively curb hallucinations when domain corpora are pre-compiled into entity hypergraphs ahead of inference [20], and clinical summarization audits confirm that permitting abstention suppresses unsupported claims [33]. Yet evaluating epistemic abstention in isolation exposes a severe utility bottleneck: strict refusal eliminates confabulation and cuts citation reasoning tokens by roughly nine-tenths, but leaves almost all protocol reference slots blank. Splitting Module 3 into parametric epistemic gating (`Module 3a`) followed by deterministic Boolean Entrez handoff and entity-anchored abstract verification (`Module 3b`) resolves both failure modes simultaneously. By forbidding the language model from guessing numeric identifiers out of weights and delegating bibliographic lookup to an exact deterministic index, the closed-loop architecture achieves complete verified citation coverage with zero ungrounded leakage and zero extra LLM tokens, a result reinforced by our blinded expert calibration showing zero false alarms in pre-registered Tier-2 auditing.

Situating mathematical rectification strictly off the data path offers a practical complement to parameter fine-tuning across closed-weight agentic ecosystems. Normative training approaches such as Bayesian teaching [21] and Gaussian-process representation optimization [35] substantially improve probabilistic belief updating and experimental selection when model weights or internal activations are accessible. In production scientific discovery platforms such as Robin [4], Co-Scientist [5], DeepEvidence [36], EvoMDT [34], HealthFlow [38], and multi-agent engineering workflows such as iDesignGPT [3], however, orchestrators routinely call proprietary commercial reasoning endpoints whose weights are frozen and whose token probabilities are hidden. **STAR-Audit** bridges these multi-agent systems with zero-resource hallucination detection [25, 29], bias-deconfounded benchmarking [37], and reproducible off-the-data-path architecture [39, 40]. Executing sub-second Newton–Raphson `OA-BTL` debiasing, selective cross-validated calibration gating, deterministic bibliographic handoff, and non-destructive `Module 4a` stream harvesting outside the stochastic prompt loop prevents both multi-agent plausible invalidity [38] and calibration degradation on natively calibrated models such as `Gemini-4-Argon-High`.

From an engineering standpoint, this architectural separation also alters the compute-versus-reliability scaling curve of autonomous agentic workflows. Conventional remediation strategies attempt to suppress errors on the data path through multi-agent debate, self-critique loops, exhaustive bidirectional tournament pairing, or iterative LLM-driven query rewriting, multiplying token consumption and wall-clock latency at every stage. Our results demonstrate that when failure modes originate from structural interface asymmetries rather than insufficient model capacity, adding further unconstrained reasoning calls is counterproductive. Replacing single-order BTL with `OA-BTL` eliminates index-order category extinction in thirty-four milliseconds without doubling pairwise API calls; replacing forced parametric citation slots with epistemic abstention and deterministic Entrez handoff eliminates nine-tenths of citation reasoning tokens while raising verified grounding to complete coverage; and applying a nested cross-validation gate before Platt scaling ensures that calibration never harms natively well-calibrated frontier models. Deterministic post-processing thus converts reliability from a token-intensive inference tax into a negligible mathematical layer.

Although validated here on biomedical target discovery and rare-disease genetics, all four **STAR-Audit** operators address structural topologies shared across general-purpose agentic AI systems. First, any multi-agent workflow that generates categorized candidates and ranks them through pairwise LLM-as-a-judge comparisons, including automated code-patch tournaments, RLHF preference arenas [8], and conceptual engineering design evaluators [3], faces index-order extinction whenever candidate pools are enumerated sequentially; inserting `OA-BTL` removes slot-position log-odds bias in milliseconds without doubling API calls through exhaustive bidirectional pairing. Second, legal case-law research, patent prior-art synthesis, and financial regulatory agents operate under identical zero-tolerance citation constraints where `Module 3a + 3b` can route parametric abstentions to deterministic court, patent, or SEC filing registries. Third, our blinded NLI audit demonstrates a general linguistic principle for multi-stage agent verification: off-the-shelf NLI cross-encoders reliably flag intra-stage judge rationalization, whereas cross-stage coherence checks must condition on speech-act polarity so that problem diagnosis and solution synthesis are not misclassified as contradictions. Fourth, non-destructive `Module 4a` stream harvesting recovers early valid solutions lost to reasoning-budget exhaustion in any multi-hop diagnostic, theorem-proving, or debugging agent, establishing a transparent quality gate aligned with FAIR data stewardship [73] and `TRIPOD+AI` reporting standards [74] before committing preclinical or engineering capital [59, 71].

Several methodological limitations define concrete priorities for prospective extension. First, we validated `OA-BTL` pathway rescue against orthogonal human-genetic, animal-model, and clinical-trial truth across dAMD, ALS, and 120 Mendelian disorders rather than re-executing prospective Step 3–6 dossier synthesis on rescued candidates or performing new wet-lab assays. While human genetic support provides a rigorous noiseless benchmark for causal target recall [59] and our synthetic Monte Carlo simulations confirm zero false-positive promotion under severe between-bucket merit skew, embedding `OA-BTL` live ahead of Step-3 expansion and testing rescued dark-genome candidates experimentally represent essential next steps. Second, whereas biomedical discovery was deliberately selected for its four objective external ground-truth anchors, direct empirical benchmarking of **STAR-Audit** on non-biomedical software engineering, legal, or design agentic pipelines remains future work, alongside hybridizing off-the-data-path post-processing with weight-level Bayesian fine-tuning [21] on open-weight backbones.

Two additional scope boundaries govern statistical resolution and external database coverage. In Block 1, executing $R = 3$ physical end-to-end discovery replicates per experimental cell avoided item-level pseudo-replication and revealed 2.81- to 4.26-fold pathway convergence, while bounding single-disease exact permutation resolution at $p = 0.10$ ($R \ge 5$ replicates are required for single-disease $p < 0.01$). Likewise, our 2,688-evaluation Gemini and Grok validation targeted stratified three-arena subsets (90 blinded pairs, 84 citation slots per arm, and 20 Mendelian diseases per model) rather than repeating all twelve full six-step Parent runs. Finally, automated Entrez verification queries official titles, abstracts, and MeSH headings rather than paywalled full-text supplements, and lexical scanning of 35 forbidden tokens eliminates surface gene or drug recitation in blind runs without erasing unlabeled pretrained pathway priors. Instrumenting autonomous multi-agent workflows with external-truth audit sensors and zero-call deterministic rectifiers ensures that scientific and decision-making AI scales on verified evidence rather than uncalibrated reasoning spin.

## Methods

### Ethical compliance and protocol governance
This computational methodology study queried publicly accessible biomedical ontologies (Human Phenotype Ontology and Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man), open bibliographic programming interfaces (NCBI Entrez PubMed), and commercial or open-weight large language model endpoints. No human participants, identifiable patient records, human biospecimens, or live vertebrate animals were involved; institutional review board approval, informed consent, and animal care committee approval were therefore not required. Prior to executing the multi-block evaluation, the complete protocol specification, frozen pathway gold standards (`APPENDIX_A_B_C_frozen_prep.md`), 120-disease HPO stimulus table (`expC_v4_pairs_120_f14.csv`), and daily canary prompts (`canary_prompts.json`) were sealed via SHA-256 cryptographic hashing (`FREEZE_MANIFEST_v1.1.json`). Twelve operational adjustments during execution (`D2-001` through `D2-012`, primarily expanding `max_tokens` from 2,048 to 32,768 and HTTP client timeouts from 300 s to 900 s after observing up to 30,000 internal reasoning tokens per high-effort call) were recorded contemporaneously and SHA-256 sealed in `DEVIATIONS_v2.md` (Supplementary Table 6). Every primary invocation ran through `gen2_call.py` (executed September to October 2026), logging 19 telemetry fields including deterministic `call_id`, UTC timestamps, `prompt_sha256`, `response_sha256`, `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `reasoning_tokens`, `latency_ms`, and `finish_reason`. Daily endpoint stability was verified via 10 fixed temperature-0 prompts (`gen2_canary.py`), confirming zero output drift (`canary_log.jsonl`).

### Block 1 six-stage discovery pipeline and blind-versus-retrieval arms
We executed 12 independent end-to-end discovery runs on `GLM-5.3` (`effort=high`, `top_p=0.95`, `temperature=0.7` for generative Steps 1, 3, and 6; `temperature=0.3` for judging Steps 2 and 4; `max_tokens=32,768`) arranged in a $2 \text{ (Disease: dAMD, ALS)} \times 2 \text{ (Arm: Blind, Retrieval)} \times 3 \text{ (Replicates: R1, R2, R3)}$ factorial design (`run_parent.py`). In Step 1 (Multi-Bucket Mechanism Ideation), the orchestrator prompted generation of 30 distinct disease mechanisms partitioned equally across `Bucket 1` (`M00–M09`, cell-intrinsic and organellar homeostasis), `Bucket 2` (`M10–M19`, intercellular signaling and neuro-immune or retinal-immune crosstalk), and `Bucket 3` (`M20–M29`, extracellular matrix, vascular barrier integrity, and systemic complement or lipid pathways), each accompanied by a verbalized confidence score ($0\text{--}100$). Prompts in the `retrieval` arm injected real PubMed abstracts retrieved via `gen2_pubmed.py`. Prompts and outputs in the `blind` arm excluded external literature and underwent automated lexical scanning (`gen2_blind.py`) against 11 dAMD and 24 ALS forbidden drug and gene tokens (Supplementary Note 1), triggering automatic redrafting (up to 4 attempts) upon any match.

In Step 2 (Tournament I Screening), 300 unordered candidate pairs were sampled without replacement from all $\binom{30}{2} = 435$ pairs using deterministic run-specific pseudorandom seeds, formatted in ascending index order $(M_a, M_b)$ with $a < b$, and judged in single-order comparisons. Candidate strengths $\pi_i$ were estimated via Hunter's minorization-maximization (MM) algorithm for the BTL model [7] with Jeffreys smoothing ($\alpha = 0.5$ pseudo-wins and $\alpha = 0.5$ pseudo-losses per compared pair), geometric-mean normalization ($\prod_{i=1}^M \pi_i = 1$), and $L_1$ convergence tolerance $10^{-9}$ (`btl.py`). Top-10 mechanisms advanced to Step 3 (Target Dossier Expansion) to specify lead HGNC gene symbols, therapeutic modulation direction, cellular readouts, and updated confidence ($0\text{--}100$). In Steps 4 and 5 (Tournament II Bidirectional Judging and Top-5 Selection), all $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ dossier pairs were judged by three independent personas (`SCIENCE`, `METHODS`, `NOVELTY`) in both forward $(A=i, B=j)$ and reverse $(A=j, B=i)$ orientations ($45 \times 3 \times 2 = 270$ scheduled calls per run; 3,202 complete bidirectional calls across 1,601 pairs and 38 singleton calls across 19 incomplete pairs), ranked via Hunter-MM BTL to select the Top 5. In Step 6, each Top-5 dossier generated a wet-lab validation protocol containing 6 structured reference slots (360 valid slots across 12 runs).

### Block 2 randomized citation trial and Block 3 Mendelian perturbation benchmark
In Block 2 (`run_expB_gen2.py`), across 14 clinical domains (`B01` Asthma through `B14` Pelizaeus–Merzbacher Disease; 5 hypotheses per domain = 70 hypotheses; Supplementary Table 3), Stage-2 citation generation on `GLM-5.3` (`temperature=0.3`, `effort=high`, `max_tokens=32,768`; 6 slots per hypothesis = 420 slots per arm; 1,260 total slots) was randomized across three arms: `as_discovered` (standard mandatory 6-reference schema), `abstain_probe` (permitting `NO_CITATION` when exact PMID recall is uncertain), and `gated` (Module 3a strict epistemic gate requiring `NO_CITATION` unless exact identifier-claim verification is guaranteed).

In Block 3 (`run_expC_v4.py`), we curated 120 Mendelian disease-gene pairs across 14 organ-system families from the Human Phenotype Ontology (`hp.obo` and `phenotype.hpoa`) [54]. Information content for each HPO term $t$ was computed as $\text{IC}(t) = -\ln(p_t)$ from OMIM empirical frequencies. Disease names and OMIM identifiers were stripped, and each profile was masked at target IC deletion doses $d \in \{0\%, 25\%, 50\%\}$ under `del_topIC` (removing highest-IC terms first) versus `del_lowIC_matched` (removing lowest-IC terms to match cumulative deleted IC within $\pm 5\%$). `GLM-5.3` and `DeepSeek-V4-Pro` (`temperature=0.6`, `effort=high`, `max_tokens=32,768`) predicted ranked Top-10 HGNC causal gene lists with confidence scores ($0\text{--}100$). Across 804 logged entries in `calls_log.jsonl`, intention-to-diagnose accounting (`nc_upgrade_A3_A7_expc_calibration.py`) separated 174 superseded pilot-ceiling retries from 630 canonical 32,768-token calls (166 baseline hits, 236 completed 10-gene misses, and 228 ceiling-truncated 0-gene misses scored conservatively as `hit@10 = 0`). HPO term-count starvation was quantified by comparing the fraction of intact clinical terms deleted between `del_lowIC_matched` and `del_topIC` via Mann–Whitney tests.

### Cross-ecosystem validation across Google Gemini and xAI Grok compute tiers
To test generalization across commercial architectures and reasoning tiers, we executed 2,688 matched evaluations across Google `Gemini-4-Argon-High` (`gemini-4-argon-high`) and `Gemini-3.8-Flash` (`gemini-3.8-flash-tiered`; 1,344 evaluations, 1,149,908 output and reasoning tokens, 12,053,103 total tokens including cache; `analyze_gemini_cross_ecosystem.py`) and xAI `Grok-4.7` (`grok-4.7`, adaptive high reasoning) and `Grok-4.7-Fast` (`grok-4.7-build-fast`, low reasoning; 1,344 evaluations, 450,879 native reasoning tokens, 1,185,113 total tokens via Grok Build CLI `v1.0.70`; `run_grok_cross_ecosystem.py`, `analyze_grok_cross_ecosystem.py`). All evaluations enforced stateless per-item prompt boundaries: in Arena 1, blinded forward (`M01`–`M45`) and shuffled reverse (`R01`–`R45`) Tournament II pairs ran in isolated contexts with zero cross-pair memory carryover alongside 720 joint-context controls; in Arena 2 (1,008 citation slots across 14 domains) and Arena 3 (240 masked HPO evaluations across 20 stratified Mendelian diseases), each hypothesis or masked phenotype profile was evaluated independently with explicit item delimiters, preventing cross-disease leakage across CLI execution chunks.

### Dimension 1 two-tier verifier, closed-loop Entrez handoff, and expert calibration
Every emitted PMID across all 2,628 slots (`step1_pubmed_verify.py`) was queried live against NCBI Entrez (`esummary` and `efetch` XML; 956 primary, 162 Gemini, and 136 Grok cached PMIDs). Tier-1 verified numeric PMID existence; frozen pre-registered Tier-2 v1 tested whether official titles, abstracts, or MeSH terms contained target disease anchors or specific mechanism tokens (>3 characters), while entity-anchored Tier-2 v2 required explicit overlap of both clinical phenotype context and core molecular or pathway entities (`nc_upgrade_A5_part1_tier2_and_cot_boxes.py`). Two immunology and neurobiology investigators independently adjudicated 100 stratified slots (`expert_audit_tier2_100_annotated.csv`) blinded to automated labels, computing precision, recall, specificity, F1, and Cohen's $\kappa$. For closed-loop **Module 3 (`Module 3a + 3b`)**, slots abstained or rejected by `Module 3a` triggered deterministic `Module 3b` NCBI Entrez `esearch` queries combining the domain MeSH term with extracted hypothesis gene or pathway symbols (`retmax=12`, sorted by relevance; 1,049 candidate records cached in `nc_upgrade_entrez_handoff_cache.json`), retaining the top record passing entity-anchored Tier-2 verification (`nc_upgrade_A2_module3_handoff.py`).

### Dimension 3 Order-Adjusted BTL, Monte Carlo identifiability, and neutralization
For ordered comparison tuples $(i_k, j_k, y_k)$ where $i_k$ occupies Slot A, $j_k$ occupies Slot B, and $y_k \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ denotes the Slot-A outcome, **Module 1 (`OA-BTL`)** maximizes the $L_2$-regularized log-likelihood (`nc_upgrade_A1_A4_btl_validation.py`):
$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \gamma) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left[ y_k \ln \sigma(\theta_{i_k} - \theta_{j_k} + \gamma) + (1 - y_k) \ln \left(1 - \sigma(\theta_{i_k} - \theta_{j_k} + \gamma)\right) \right] - \frac{\lambda}{2} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \theta_m^2$$
subject to $\sum_{m=1}^{M} \theta_m = 0$ ($\lambda = 0.01$, swept across $\lambda \in [10^{-4}, 1.0]$). Newton–Raphson Hessian updates jointly estimate de-biased candidate merits $\hat{\theta}_m$ and the Slot-A log-odds intercept $\hat{\gamma}$ ($\text{SE}(\hat{\gamma}) = \sqrt{[H^{-1}]_{\gamma\gamma}}$). To test identifiability under strict $a < b$ index-bucket ordering, we also fitted a within-bucket-anchored estimator restricting $\gamma$ estimation to the 1,079 intra-bucket pairs, and conducted 400-simulation synthetic Monte Carlo experiments ($M = 30, K = 300$) across $\gamma_{\text{true}} \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$ and confounded null regimes ($\gamma_{\text{true}} = 0$ with Bucket-1 merit shift $\Delta_{\text{B1-B3}} \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\}$).

In Tournament II, we benchmarked five aggregation rules across all 12 runs: unadjusted raw BTL on all 3,240 calls, complete-pair symmetrized BTL (`1:1` weight 2 on flips, excluding 19 incomplete singleton pairs), neutral-tie reweighted BTL (`0.5:0.5` weight 1 on flipped pairs), strict discordance-dropped BTL (retaining only order-consistent pairs), majority voting, and Elo ($K = 32$). Representative Tournament I dAMD rescues comprised `M23` (`GAS6/PROS1–MERTK` in `dAMD_retrieval_R1`, 14/16 Slot B, Rank 11 to 7), `M25` (`TIMP3/MMP2/MMP9` in `dAMD_retrieval_R2`, 15/19 Slot B, Rank 15 to 8), `M25` (`ABCA1/LIPC/CETP` in `dAMD_retrieval_R3`, 15/19 Slot B, Rank 15 to 6), `M14` (`CD59` in `dAMD_retrieval_R3`, 9/18 Slot B, Rank 11 to 5), and `M22` (`MTTP/APOB100` in `dAMD_blind_R1`, 15/21 Slot B, Rank 12 to 6). Corresponding ALS rescues comprised `M26` (`MMP9/LAMA1` in `ALS_blind_R2`, 17/19 Slot B, Rank 13 to 6), `M29` (`CCS/ATP7A` in `ALS_retrieval_R3`, 23/23 Slot B, Rank 15 to 10), `M25` (`MCT1/SLC16A1` in `ALS_blind_R3`, 15/19 Slot B, Rank 11 to 7), and `M18` (`C1QA/C3` in `ALS_blind_R1`, 13/23 Slot B, Rank 13 to 10; Supplementary Table 2).

### Dimensions 4–6 bootstrap, NLI audit, selective calibration, and CoT-SRAD
Dimension 4 resampled each run's 270 Tournament II verdicts 10,000 times with replacement, refitting Hunter-MM BTL per draw to construct the verdict-noise null for Top-5 Jaccard overlap. Dimension 5 applied `MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli` [57] across 480 de-aliased `SCIENCE` judge rationale pairs and 1,766 same-entity cross-stage pairs (`nc_upgrade_A6_A9_parity_and_timing.py`), sweeping contradiction threshold $\tau \in [0.50, 0.95]$ around pre-registered $\tau = 0.75$ (strict bidirectional $\min > \tau$; directional $\max > \tau$). Blinded dual-expert review of 100 NLI pairs (`expert_audit_nli_100_annotated.csv`; 50 Tournament II, 50 cross-stage) quantified precision, recall, F1, $\kappa$, and speech-act polarity filter reduction (`nc_upgrade_A5_part2_nli_audit.py`). Dimension 6 estimated run-clustered calibration slope $\hat{\beta}_{\text{cal}}$ on $\text{logit}(\text{clip}(c_i/100, 0.01, 0.99))$ and benchmarked **Module 2 (`Selective LORO/LODO Platt Scaling`)** [23, 72] against intercept-only shift, temperature scaling, shrunk Platt, and isotonic regression (`nc_upgrade_A3_A7_expc_calibration.py`): two-parameter Platt scaling $\hat{p}_i = \sigma(a_{-r}\text{logit}(\tilde{c}_i) + b_{-r})$ was applied out-of-sample if and only if held-in training folds exceeded $\text{ECE} > 0.12$ (swept across $[0.08, 0.15]$), preventing small-sample overfitting on well-calibrated models.

Pipeline-level composite trustworthiness in Fig. 6D, E was defined in closed form by normalizing Bucket-3 survival to its 1/3 uniform benchmark ($100 \times \min(1, 3 \cdot \text{Bucket3Share})$) and averaging equally with Tournament II order-consistent weight (%), closed-loop verified grounded citation yield (%), and calibration accuracy ($100 - 100\cdot\text{ECE}$), reported alongside the unnormalized arithmetic mean $S_{\text{raw}}$. For **Module 4 (`CoT-SRAD`)**, we constructed a strictly external zero-leakage vocabulary of 3,871 Mendelian HGNC symbols from `expC_candidate_pool.csv` (alongside the 1,237-symbol emitted prediction vocabulary) and computed Leave-One-Disease-Out prediction document frequency $\text{df}_{-d}(g)$ across diseases $d' \neq d$. Five-way ablation compared baseline predictions, non-destructive `Module 4a` (backfilling incomplete ceiling-truncated calls in order of earliest `reasoning_content` activation while banning ubiquitous attractors $\text{df}_{-d}(g) \ge \tau_{\text{ban}}$), `Module 4b` (overwriting completed 10-gene lists), unbanned stream harvesting, and `4a + 4b` across $\tau_{\text{ban}} \in \{6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20\}$ and minimum stream mentions $\in \{1, 2, 3\}$. On single-thread CPU hardware, `OA-BTL` required 34.0 ms and 0.10 MB peak RAM per 300-pair run, selective calibration and `CoT-SRAD` required <5 ms per run, and `DeBERTa-v3` NLI required 389.6 ms per bidirectional pair. All analyses used Python (v3.11/v3.14), NumPy (v2.4.4), SciPy (v1.16.3), Statsmodels (v0.14.6), PyTorch (v2.12.0), Transformers (v4.47.0), and Matplotlib (v3.10.9), controlling confirmatory multiplicity via Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at $q = 0.05$ [41] with 10,000-draw cluster bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (`domain_id`, `run_id`, `disease_id`).
